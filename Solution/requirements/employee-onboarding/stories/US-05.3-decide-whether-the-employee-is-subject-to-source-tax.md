# US-05.3 – Decide whether the employee is subject to source tax

Part of [Employee onboarding questionnaire](../feature.md), section [5. Who is subject to source tax](../feature.md#SEC-05-source-tax-liability).

**As** source tax office, **I want** Lonio to decide for every employee, from the answers given, whether they are subject to source tax, following HR's consolidated rule, **so that** only employees subject to source tax answer the tariff questions and get a tariff code, and nobody is wrongly left out or included

Priority **P1** – HR's own rule changed several times during the interview and HR never knows up front who has to answer the source tax questions; a wrong decision means wrong withholding (F-hr_transcript-b0527c, F-hr_transcript-845204, F-hr_transcript-b84498). · Status: **decision** · Sources: F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a, F-hr_transcript-b0527c, F-hr_transcript-845204, F-hr_transcript-b90bd9, F-hr_transcript-b84498, G-01, D-003, D-007, D-008, D-037, D-038, D-041, D-043, D-045

Covers (details in [the section](../feature.md#SEC-05-source-tax-liability) and [information.md](../information.md)): Subject to source tax

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-05.3.1 Employees living abroad are always subject**
  - **Given** an employee's home address on the first working day is in a country other than Switzerland
  - **When** Lonio decides source tax liability
  - **Then** the employee is subject to source tax whatever their nationality, even a Swiss citizen such as Stefan. 'Here' in HR's wording is read as Switzerland (decision D-043, an assumption to be verified by a domain expert). How Liechtenstein residents are then treated under the double taxation agreement is decided by the Liechtenstein questions, not by this rule.
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, D-038, D-041, D-043, D-007_

  | Example | Country (of residence) | → | Subject to source tax | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Swiss citizen living abroad, like Stefan (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a) (illustrative) | Germany | → | yes | ✅ |
  | Employee living in Liechtenstein (illustrative) | Liechtenstein | → | yes | ✅ |
  | Employee living in another country (illustrative) | France | → | yes | ✅ |

- **AC-05.3.2 Swiss citizens and C permit holders living in Switzerland are not subject**
  - **Given** an employee lives in Switzerland
  - **When** the employee is Swiss (including anyone who also holds another nationality) or holds a C permit
  - **Then** the employee is not subject to source tax. Treating dual nationals with Swiss citizenship as Swiss is decision D-008, an assumption to be verified by a domain expert.
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-b0527c, F-hr_transcript-845204, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, D-008, D-043_

  | Example | Country (of residence) | Nationality | Holds a C permit | → | Subject to source tax | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Maria (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-845204) | Switzerland | Portugal | yes | → | no | ✅ |
  | Swiss employee (also a dual national) (illustrative) | Switzerland | Switzerland | – | → | no | ✅ |

- **AC-05.3.3 Non-Swiss residents without C permit who are not married are subject**
  - **Given** an employee lives in Switzerland, is not Swiss and has no C permit
  - **When** the civil status is Single, Divorced, Separated or Widowed
  - **Then** the employee is subject to source tax. Separated counts as single for source tax (decision D-003, an assumption to be verified by a domain expert).
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, D-003, D-043_

  | Example | Country (of residence) | Nationality | Holds a C permit | Civil status | → | Subject to source tax | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Single, non-Swiss without C permit (illustrative) | Switzerland | Germany | no | Single | → | yes | ✅ |
  | Separated, non-Swiss without C permit (illustrative) | Switzerland | Germany | no | Separated | → | yes | ✅ |
  | Widowed, non-Swiss without C permit (illustrative) | Switzerland | Germany | no | Widowed | → | yes | ✅ |

- **AC-05.3.4 Spouse exemption for married employees and registered partners**
  - **Given** an employee lives in Switzerland, is not Swiss, has no C permit and is Married or in a Registered partnership
  - **When** Lonio checks the spouse or registered partner
  - **Then** the employee is not subject to source tax if the spouse is Swiss or holds a C permit and lives in Switzerland. The employee is subject if the spouse is Swiss or a C permit holder but does not live in Switzerland, or if the spouse is neither Swiss nor a C permit holder. Registered partnership counts as married (decision D-003, an assumption to be verified by a domain expert).
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-b90bd9, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, D-003, D-043_

  | Example | Country (of residence) | Nationality | Holds a C permit | Civil status | Spouse's nationality | Spouse's country of residence | Spouse holds a C permit | → | Subject to source tax | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Tomás (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-b90bd9) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Married | Switzerland | Switzerland | – | → | no | ✅ |
  | Registered partner with C permit living in Switzerland (illustrative) | Switzerland | Germany | no | Registered partnership | Italy | Switzerland | yes | → | no | ✅ |
  | Swiss spouse living abroad (illustrative) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Married | Switzerland | Germany | – | → | yes | ✅ |
  | Spouse neither Swiss nor C permit holder (illustrative) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Married | Italy | – | no | → | yes | ✅ |

- **AC-05.3.5 Result drives the tariff questions and is derived again from the latest answers**
  - **Given** Lonio has decided whether an employee is subject to source tax
  - **When** the questionnaire continues, or an answer it depends on is later changed by HR
  - **Then** only employees subject to source tax are asked the tariff questions, and the result is always derived again from the latest answers (decision D-037); employees not subject are not asked them.
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-b84498, G-01, D-001, D-037_

## Evidence
- **D-001** (product decision (Product owner)): Lonio computes the full Zurich source tax tariff code (tariff letter including the cross-border letters, child digit and church-tax suffix, e.g. "C1Y", "L0Y") for every employee subject to source tax, from the answers it collects, following the calculator's logic. HR no longer clicks through the calculator.
- **D-003** (product decision (Product owner)): Civil status offers Single, Married, Divorced, Widowed, Registered partnership and Separated. For source tax, registered partnership counts as married (including the spouse exemption) and separated counts as single, as in the calculator. For the pension fund, registered partnership is entered as Married with the partner's date of birth, and separated as Married. This closes OQ-civil-status-partnership. Assumption - to be verified by a domain expert; state this assumption in the user story.
- **D-007** (product decision (Product owner)): Liechtenstein residents are asked the source tax questions (HR's rule decides who is asked). The result follows the calculator - with daily return or at most 45 non-return days, Lonio shows 'in principle no source tax liability, subject to the double taxation agreement' instead of a tariff code, flagged for HR; with more than 45 non-return days, the normal tariff code. This closes OQ-liechtenstein-conflict. To be verified by a domain expert.
- **D-008** (product decision (Product owner)): Anyone with Swiss citizenship counts as Swiss for source tax, even with another nationality. This closes OQ-dual-nationality. To be verified by a domain expert.
- **D-037** (product decision (Product owner)): After submission the questionnaire is read-only for the employee. Every derived result (insurance start date, civil status for the fund and for source tax, subject to source tax, tariff code) is always derived again from the latest answers, as for the first working day (D-033).
