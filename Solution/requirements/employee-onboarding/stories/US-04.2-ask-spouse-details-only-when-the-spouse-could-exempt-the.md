# US-04.2 – Ask spouse details only when the spouse could exempt the employee

Part of [Employee onboarding questionnaire](../feature.md), section [4. Source tax liability check](../feature.md#SEC-04-source-tax-liability).

**As** source tax office, **I want** to know whether a married or registered-partner employee's spouse or partner is Swiss or holds a C permit, and whether they live in Switzerland, but only when this can change the employee's source tax liability, **so that** employees exempted through their spouse, such as Tomás, are not wrongly treated as subject to source tax, and no spouse data is collected when it cannot change the result

Priority **P1** – HR's real case Tomás shows that leaving out the spouse leads to the wrong liability decision (F-hr_transcript-d7431e). HR states which spouse facts are needed (F-hr_transcript-f3ce84). · Status: **inferred** · Sources: F-hr_transcript-f3ce84, F-hr_transcript-d7431e, F-hr_transcript-b6d9b3, F-zh_source_tax_calculator-051bb9, G-01, D-003, D-005, D-008, D-036

Covers (details in [the section](../feature.md#SEC-04-source-tax-liability) and [information.md](../information.md)): Spouse's Swiss citizenship, Spouse holds a C permit, Spouse lives in Switzerland, Spouse exemption from source tax

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-04.2.1 Spouse's Swiss citizenship is asked only when the spouse could exempt the employee**
  - **Given** the employee lives in Switzerland, is not a Swiss citizen and holds no C permit
  - **When** the employee's civil status is Married or Registered partnership
  - **Then** the employee is asked whether their spouse or registered partner holds Swiss citizenship ("yes" or "no"; dual citizens count as "yes") and must answer. It is not asked of Single, Divorced, Widowed or Separated employees, because separated employees count as single for source tax. It is not asked of employees who are Swiss, hold a C permit or live abroad. The answer is recorded for source tax.
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-f3ce84, F-hr_transcript-d7431e, F-hr_transcript-b6d9b3, D-003, D-005, D-008, G-01_

  | Example | Country of home address (country of residence) | Swiss citizenship | C permit held | Civil status | → | Spouse's Swiss citizenship | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Tomás (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-d7431e) | Switzerland | no | no | Married | → | required | ✅ |
  | Registered partnership, non-Swiss without C permit (illustrative) | Switzerland | no | no | Registered partnership | → | asked | ✅ |
  | Separated, non-Swiss without C permit (illustrative) | Switzerland | no | no | Separated | → | not asked | ✅ |
  | Divorced, non-Swiss without C permit (illustrative) | Switzerland | no | no | Divorced | → | not asked | ✅ |
  | Married, non-Swiss with C permit (illustrative) | Switzerland | no | yes | Married | → | not asked | ✅ |
  | Married Swiss citizen living in Switzerland (illustrative) | Switzerland | yes | – | Married | → | not asked | ✅ |
  | Married employee living in Germany (illustrative) | Germany | – | – | Married | → | not asked | ✅ |

- **AC-04.2.2 Spouse's C permit is asked only for a non-Swiss spouse**
  - **Given** the employee has been asked about their spouse's or partner's Swiss citizenship
  - **When** the employee answers that the spouse or partner is not a Swiss citizen
  - **Then** the employee is asked whether the spouse or partner holds a C permit (permanent residence), with the answers "yes" or "no", and must answer. No other permit type is asked. When the spouse is Swiss, this question is not asked. The answer is recorded for source tax.
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-f3ce84, F-hr_transcript-d7431e, D-005, G-01_

  | Example | Country of home address (country of residence) | Swiss citizenship | C permit held | Civil status | Spouse's Swiss citizenship | → | Spouse holds a C permit | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Non-Swiss spouse (illustrative) | Switzerland | no | no | Married | no | → | required | ✅ |
  | Tomás - Swiss wife (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-d7431e) | Switzerland | no | no | Married | yes | → | not asked | ✅ |

- **AC-04.2.3 Spouse's residence is asked only for a Swiss or C-permit spouse**
  - **Given** the employee has stated that their spouse or partner is a Swiss citizen or holds a C permit
  - **When** the spouse questions continue
  - **Then** the employee is asked whether the spouse or partner lives in Switzerland, with the answers "Switzerland" or "Another country", and must answer. The spouse's actual country is not collected. When the spouse is neither Swiss nor holds a C permit, the question is not asked, because the spouse cannot exempt the employee. The answer is recorded for source tax.
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-f3ce84, F-hr_transcript-d7431e, F-hr_transcript-b6d9b3, G-01_

  | Example | Country of home address (country of residence) | Swiss citizenship | C permit held | Civil status | Spouse's Swiss citizenship | Spouse holds a C permit | → | Spouse lives in Switzerland | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Tomás - Swiss wife (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-d7431e) | Switzerland | no | no | Married | yes | – | → | required | ✅ |
  | Non-Swiss spouse with C permit (illustrative) | Switzerland | no | no | Married | no | yes | → | asked | ✅ |
  | Non-Swiss spouse without C permit (illustrative) | Switzerland | no | no | Married | no | no | → | not asked | ✅ |

- **AC-04.2.4 Spouse exemption is decided from civil status and the spouse answers**
  - **Given** the employee has answered the civil status question and every spouse question that was asked
  - **When** Lonio decides the spouse exemption
  - **Then** the employee is exempt only if they are Married or in a Registered partnership and their spouse or partner is a Swiss citizen or holds a C permit and lives in Switzerland. In every other case there is no exemption, including Single, Divorced, Widowed and Separated employees, a spouse living in another country, and a non-Swiss spouse without a C permit.
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-d7431e, F-hr_transcript-b6d9b3, F-hr_transcript-f3ce84, F-zh_source_tax_calculator-051bb9, D-003, D-005, D-008_

  | Example | Country of home address (country of residence) | Swiss citizenship | C permit held | Civil status | Spouse's Swiss citizenship | Spouse lives in Switzerland | Spouse holds a C permit | → | Spouse exemption from source tax | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Tomás - Swiss wife living in Winterthur (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-d7431e) | Switzerland | no | no | Married | yes | Switzerland | – | → | yes | ✅ |
  | Registered partner is Swiss and lives in Switzerland (illustrative) | Switzerland | no | no | Registered partnership | yes | Switzerland | – | → | yes | ✅ |
  | Spouse with C permit living in Switzerland (illustrative) | Switzerland | no | no | Married | no | Switzerland | yes | → | yes | ✅ |
  | Swiss spouse living in another country (illustrative) | Switzerland | no | no | Married | yes | Another country | – | → | no | ✅ |
  | C-permit spouse living in another country (illustrative) | Switzerland | no | no | Married | no | Another country | yes | → | no | ✅ |
  | Non-Swiss spouse without C permit (illustrative) | Switzerland | no | no | Married | no | – | no | → | no | ✅ |
  | Single employee (illustrative) | Switzerland | no | no | Single | – | – | – | → | no | ✅ |
  | Separated employee (counts as single) (illustrative) | Switzerland | no | no | Separated | – | – | – | → | no | ✅ |

- **AC-04.2.5 Spouse answers that no longer apply are not kept**
  - **Given** the employee has answered spouse questions
  - **When** they change an earlier answer so that the spouse questions no longer apply (for example civil status changed to Single, or they now answer that they hold a C permit) and then submit the questionnaire
  - **Then** those spouse answers are kept while the employee goes back and forth, but are discarded on submission. Only answers to questions that still apply are recorded.
  - _Sources: D-036, G-01_

## Evidence
- **D-003** (product decision (Product owner)): Civil status offers Single, Married, Divorced, Widowed, Registered partnership and Separated. For source tax, registered partnership counts as married (including the spouse exemption) and separated counts as single, as in the calculator. For the pension fund, provisionally, registered partnership is entered as Married with the partner's date of birth, and separated as Married. Keep OQ-civil-status-partnership open as non-blocking, to be verified by a domain expert.
- **D-005** (product decision (Product owner)): Do not ask for the permit type. Ask non-Swiss employees only whether they hold a C permit (permanent residence), yes or no, and the same for a non-Swiss spouse. No party needs any other permit type. This closes OQ-permit-list. To be verified by a domain expert.
- **D-008** (product decision (Product owner)): Anyone with Swiss citizenship counts as Swiss for source tax, even with another nationality. This closes OQ-dual-nationality. To be verified by a domain expert.
- **D-036** (product decision (Product owner)): Answers to questions that no longer apply after the employee changes an earlier answer are discarded on submission; until then they may be kept so going back and forth loses no input.
- **F-hr_transcript-b6d9b3** (hr_transcript): Consolidated final rule: an employee is subject to source tax if they live abroad (home address country not Switzerland), regardless of nationality. If they live in Switzerland, they are subject only if they are not Swiss and do not hold a C permit, unless they are married to a spouse who is Swiss or holds a C permit and lives in Switzerland (then not subject).
  - "If you don't have a Swiss passport, you do source tax." — hr_transcript/T-010, Anna
  - "So, not the people with a C permit." — hr_transcript/T-038, Anna
  - "If you're married to someone Swiss, or to someone with a C permit, and that person lives here, then you don't do source tax either." — hr_transcript/T-044, Anna
  - "Those people do source tax because they work here and they don't live here." — hr_transcript/T-048, Anna
- **F-hr_transcript-d7431e** (hr_transcript): An employee married to someone Swiss or to someone with a C permit, where that spouse lives in Switzerland, is not subject to source tax (e.g. Tomás: Brazilian, B permit, Swiss wife, living in Winterthur → no source tax).
  - "If you're married to someone Swiss, or to someone with a C permit, and that person lives here, then you don't do source tax either." — hr_transcript/T-044, Anna
  - "Brazilian, B permit, but his wife is Swiss, and they live in Winterthur. So no source tax for him." — hr_transcript/T-044, Anna
- **F-hr_transcript-f3ce84** (hr_transcript): For married employees, the spouse's nationality, permit (if any) and whether the spouse lives in Switzerland are needed to determine source tax liability.
  - "So you need to know about the spouse as well." — hr_transcript/T-045, Leo
  - "Yes. Their nationality, their permit if they have one, and that they live here." — hr_transcript/T-046, Anna
- **F-zh_source_tax_calculator-051bb9** (zh_source_tax_calculator): Civil status has two options: 'Verheiratet oder eingetragene Partnerschaft' (married or registered partnership) and 'Ledig, Geschieden, Getrennt, Verwitwet' (single, divorced, separated, widowed).
  - "Options: Verheiratet oder eingetragene Partnerschaft | Ledig, Geschieden, Getrennt, Verwitwet" — zh_source_tax_calculator/C-Q02
- **G-01** (product owner guidance): Lonio collects onboarding data from a new employee **once** and reuses it for every receiving party. Asking too little blocks a registration; asking too much collects personal data we must not have (data minimisation). A question must only be asked when at least one receiving party needs the answer for this person.
