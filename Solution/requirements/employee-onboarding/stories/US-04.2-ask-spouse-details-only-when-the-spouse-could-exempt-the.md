# US-04.2 – Ask spouse details only when the spouse could exempt the employee

Part of [Employee onboarding questionnaire](../feature.md), section [4. Source tax liability check](../feature.md#SEC-04-source-tax-liability).

**As** new employee, **I want** to be asked about my spouse or registered partner's residence, nationality and C permit only when these answers can change whether I am subject to source tax, **so that** I give no personal data about my spouse that no party needs, while a Swiss or C-permit spouse living in Switzerland still exempts me from source tax

Priority **P1** – Without the spouse details HR wrongly treats exempt employees like Tomás as subject to source tax; asking them of everyone would violate data minimisation (G-01). · Status: **inferred** · Sources: F-hr_transcript-d7431e, F-hr_transcript-f3ce84, F-hr_transcript-b6d9b3, G-01, D-003, D-005, D-008, D-036

> ⚠ Open questions affecting this section: [OQ-nationality-scope](../open-questions.md#oq-nationality-scope)
Covers (details in [the section](../feature.md#SEC-04-source-tax-liability) and [information.md](../information.md)): Spouse's / registered partner's country of residence, Spouse's / registered partner's nationality, Spouse / registered partner holds a C permit, Spouse exemption from source tax

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-04.2.1 Spouse's country of residence is asked only when the exemption can matter**
  - **Given** the employee lives in Switzerland, is not Swiss, holds no C permit and has given their civil status
  - **When** the civil status is Married or Registered partnership
  - **Then** the employee is asked in which country their spouse or registered partner lives (countries by English name) and must answer it; it is not asked of single, divorced, widowed or separated employees (separated counts as single for source tax), nor of employees who are Swiss, hold a C permit or live abroad. See OQ-nationality-scope on whether only 'lives in Switzerland yes/no' is needed.
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-d7431e, F-hr_transcript-f3ce84, F-hr_transcript-b6d9b3, G-01, D-003_

  | Example | Country (home address) = country of residence | Nationality | Holds a C permit | Civil status | → | Spouse's / registered partner's country of residence | Checked |

  | Tomás (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-d7431e) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Married | → | required | ✅ |
  | Registered partner without C permit (illustrative) | Switzerland | Italy | no | Registered partnership | → | asked | ✅ |
  | Separated employee without C permit (illustrative) | Switzerland | Italy | no | Separated | → | not asked | ✅ |
  | Divorced employee without C permit (illustrative) | Switzerland | Italy | no | Divorced | → | not asked | ✅ |

  | Married Swiss employee (illustrative) | Switzerland | Switzerland | – | Married | → | not asked | ✅ |
  | Married employee living abroad (illustrative) | Germany | – | – | Married | → | not asked | ✅ |

- **AC-04.2.2 Spouse's nationality is asked only if the spouse lives in Switzerland**
  - **Given** the employee was asked where their spouse or registered partner lives
  - **When** the spouse lives in Switzerland
  - **Then** the employee is asked for the spouse's nationality (countries by English name; anyone with Swiss citizenship counts as Swiss) and must answer it; if the spouse lives abroad, neither the spouse's nationality nor C permit is asked
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-f3ce84, F-hr_transcript-d7431e, G-01, D-008_

  | Example | Country (home address) = country of residence | Nationality | Holds a C permit | Civil status | Spouse's / registered partner's country of residence | → | Spouse's / registered partner's nationality | Spouse / registered partner holds a C permit | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Tomás (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-d7431e) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Married | Switzerland | → | required | – | ✅ |
  | Spouse living abroad (illustrative) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Married | Brazil | → | not asked | not asked | ✅ |

- **AC-04.2.3 Spouse's C permit is asked only if the spouse is not Swiss**
  - **Given** the spouse or registered partner lives in Switzerland
  - **When** the spouse is not Swiss
  - **Then** the employee is asked whether the spouse holds a C permit (permanent residence), answered yes or no, and must answer it; no other permit type is asked; for a Swiss spouse the question is not asked
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-f3ce84, F-hr_transcript-d7431e, D-005_

  | Example | Country (home address) = country of residence | Nationality | Holds a C permit | Civil status | Spouse's / registered partner's country of residence | Spouse's / registered partner's nationality | → | Spouse / registered partner holds a C permit | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Tomás (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-d7431e) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Married | Switzerland | Switzerland | → | not asked | ✅ |
  | Non-Swiss spouse living in Switzerland (illustrative) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Married | Switzerland | Portugal | → | required | ✅ |

- **AC-04.2.4 Spouse exemption from source tax**
  - **Given** the employee's civil status and, where asked, the spouse's country of residence, nationality and C permit are known
  - **When** Lonio checks the spouse exemption
  - **Then** the employee is exempted only if married or in a registered partnership with a spouse or partner who lives in Switzerland and is Swiss or holds a C permit; a spouse living abroad, a non-Swiss spouse without a C permit, and separated, single, divorced or widowed employees give no exemption
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-d7431e, F-hr_transcript-b6d9b3, F-hr_transcript-f3ce84, D-003, D-005, D-008_

  | Example | Country (home address) = country of residence | Nationality | Holds a C permit | Civil status | Spouse's / registered partner's country of residence | Spouse's / registered partner's nationality | Spouse / registered partner holds a C permit | → | Spouse exemption from source tax | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Tomás (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-d7431e) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Married | Switzerland | Switzerland | – | → | yes | ✅ |
  | Registered partner with C permit living in Switzerland (illustrative) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Registered partnership | Switzerland | Portugal | yes | → | yes | ✅ |
  | Non-Swiss spouse without C permit (illustrative) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Married | Switzerland | Portugal | no | → | no | ✅ |

  | Separated employee (illustrative) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Separated | – | – | – | → | no | ✅ |
  | Single employee (illustrative) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Single | – | – | – | → | no | ✅ |

- **AC-04.2.5 Spouse answers that no longer apply are not recorded**
  - **Given** the employee has answered spouse questions and then changes an earlier answer (e.g. to holding a C permit, or to a civil status other than Married or Registered partnership) so that the spouse questions no longer apply
  - **When** the questionnaire is submitted
  - **Then** the earlier spouse answers are discarded and not recorded for any party; before submission they may be kept so that going back and forth loses no input
  - _Sources: D-036, G-01_

## Evidence
- **D-003** (product decision (Product owner)): Civil status offers Single, Married, Divorced, Widowed, Registered partnership and Separated. For source tax, registered partnership counts as married (including the spouse exemption) and separated counts as single, as in the calculator. For the pension fund, provisionally, registered partnership is entered as Married with the partner's date of birth, and separated as Married. Keep OQ-civil-status-partnership open as non-blocking, to be verified by a domain expert.
- **D-005** (product decision (Product owner)): Do not ask for the permit type. Ask non-Swiss employees only whether they hold a C permit (permanent residence), yes or no, and the same for a non-Swiss spouse. No party needs any other permit type. This closes OQ-permit-list. To be verified by a domain expert.
- **D-008** (product decision (Product owner)): Anyone with Swiss citizenship counts as Swiss for source tax, even with another nationality. This closes OQ-dual-nationality. To be verified by a domain expert.
- **D-036** (product decision (Product owner)): Answers to questions that no longer apply after the employee changes an earlier answer are discarded on submission; until then they may be kept so going back and forth loses no input.
- **F-hr_transcript-b6d9b3** (hr_transcript, inferred): Consolidated final rule: an employee is subject to source tax if they live abroad (home address country not Switzerland), regardless of nationality. If they live in Switzerland, they are subject only if they are not Swiss and do not hold a C permit, unless they are married to a spouse who is Swiss or holds a C permit and lives in Switzerland (then not subject).
  - "If you don't have a Swiss passport, you do source tax." — hr_transcript/T-010, Anna
  - "So, not the people with a C permit." — hr_transcript/T-038, Anna
  - "If you're married to someone Swiss, or to someone with a C permit, and that person lives here, then you don't do source tax either." — hr_transcript/T-044, Anna
  - "Those people do source tax because they work here and they don't live here." — hr_transcript/T-048, Anna
- **F-hr_transcript-d7431e** (hr_transcript): An employee married to someone Swiss or to someone with a C permit, where that spouse lives in Switzerland, is not subject to source tax (e.g. Tomás: Brazilian, B permit, Swiss wife, living in Winterthur → no source tax).
  - "If you're married to someone Swiss, or to someone with a C permit, and that person lives here, then you don't do source tax either." — hr_transcript/T-044, Anna
  - "Brazilian, B permit, but his wife is Swiss, and they live in Winterthur. So no source tax for him." — hr_transcript/T-044, Anna
- **F-hr_transcript-f3ce84** (hr_transcript): For married employees, the spouse's nationality, permit (if any) and whether the spouse lives in Switzerland are needed to determine source tax liability.
  - "So you need to know about the spouse as well." — hr_transcript/T-045, Leo
  - "Yes. Their nationality, their permit if they have one, and that they live here." — hr_transcript/T-046, Anna
- **G-01** (product owner guidance): Lonio collects onboarding data from a new employee **once** and reuses it for every receiving party. Asking too little blocks a registration; asking too much collects personal data we must not have (data minimisation). A question must only be asked when at least one receiving party needs the answer for this person.
