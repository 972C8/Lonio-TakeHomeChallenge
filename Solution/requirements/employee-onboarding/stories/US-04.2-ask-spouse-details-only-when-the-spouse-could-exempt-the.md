# US-04.2 – Ask spouse details only when the spouse could exempt the employee

Part of [Employee onboarding questionnaire](../feature.md), section [4. Source tax liability check](../feature.md#SEC-04-source-tax-liability).

**As** HR, **I want** Lonio to ask about the spouse or registered partner (where they live, their nationality, their C permit) only when that can exempt the employee from source tax, **so that** employees like Tomás, married to a Swiss wife living in Switzerland, are correctly found not subject to source tax, without asking every married employee about their spouse

Priority **P1** – The spouse exemption decides source tax liability. A wrong decision gives a wrong tariff or asks source tax questions of people who do not need them. Data minimisation (G-01) limits the spouse questions to the cases where they decide something. · Status: **inferred** · Sources: F-hr_transcript-f3ce84, F-hr_transcript-d7431e, F-hr_transcript-b6d9b3, G-01, G-10, D-003, D-005, D-008

> ⚠ Open questions affecting this section: [OQ-nationality-wording](../open-questions.md#oq-nationality-wording)
Covers (details in [the section](../feature.md#SEC-04-source-tax-liability) and [information.md](../information.md)): Spouse's / partner's country of residence, Spouse's / partner's nationality, Spouse / partner holds a C permit, Spouse exempts from source tax

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-04.2.1 Spouse's country of residence is asked first, and only when it can matter**
  - **Given** the employee lives in Switzerland, is not Swiss and has answered "no" to holding a C permit
  - **When** the employee's civil status is Married or Registered partnership
  - **Then** the employee is asked in which country their spouse or registered partner lives, given as a country by its English name, and must answer it. It is recorded for source tax. It is not asked of single, divorced, widowed or separated employees, because separated counts as single for source tax. It is also not asked of employees who are Swiss, hold a C permit or live abroad, because the spouse cannot change their liability. Asking the residence before nationality and permit is inferred from data minimisation: a spouse living outside Switzerland never exempts.
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-f3ce84, F-hr_transcript-b6d9b3, G-01, G-10, D-003_

  | Example | Country (home address) | Nationality | Holds a C permit (permanent residence) | Civil status | → | Spouse's / partner's country of residence | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Tomás, Brazilian, B permit, married (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-d7431e) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Married | → | required | ✅ |
  | Registered partner without C permit (illustrative) | Switzerland | Germany | no | Registered partnership | → | asked | ✅ |
  | Separated employee without C permit (illustrative) | Switzerland | Germany | no | Separated | → | not asked | ✅ |
  | Married employee with C permit (illustrative) | Switzerland | Germany | yes | Married | → | not asked | ✅ |
  | Married employee living abroad (illustrative) | Germany | – | – | Married | → | not asked | ✅ |

- **AC-04.2.2 Spouse's nationality is asked only if the spouse lives in Switzerland**
  - **Given** the employee has been asked where their spouse or registered partner lives
  - **When** the spouse lives in Switzerland
  - **Then** the employee is asked for the spouse's nationality, given as a country by its English name, and must answer it. A spouse holding Swiss citizenship is entered as "Switzerland", even with another nationality. Applying the dual-citizenship rule to the spouse is an inference (see OQ-nationality-wording). It is recorded for source tax. If the spouse lives in any other country, neither the spouse's nationality nor their C permit is asked.
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-f3ce84, F-hr_transcript-d7431e, G-10, D-008_

  | Example | Country (home address) | Nationality | Holds a C permit (permanent residence) | Civil status | Spouse's / partner's country of residence | → | Spouse's / partner's nationality | Spouse / partner holds a C permit | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Tomás, wife lives in Winterthur (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-d7431e) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Married | Switzerland | → | required | – | ✅ |
  | Spouse lives in Germany (illustrative) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Married | Germany | → | not asked | not asked | ✅ |

- **AC-04.2.3 Spouse's C permit is asked only for a non-Swiss spouse living in Switzerland**
  - **Given** the spouse or registered partner lives in Switzerland and the employee has given the spouse's nationality
  - **When** the spouse's nationality is not Switzerland
  - **Then** the employee is asked whether the spouse holds a C permit, answered with "yes" or "no", and must answer it. It is recorded for source tax. It is not asked for a Swiss spouse, and the spouse's other permit types are never asked.
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-f3ce84, D-005_

  | Example | Country (home address) | Nationality | Holds a C permit (permanent residence) | Civil status | Spouse's / partner's country of residence | Spouse's / partner's nationality | → | Spouse / partner holds a C permit | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Tomás, Swiss wife (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-d7431e) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Married | Switzerland | Switzerland | → | not asked | ✅ |
  | Italian spouse living in Switzerland (illustrative) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Married | Switzerland | Italy | → | required | ✅ |

- **AC-04.2.4 Lonio decides whether the spouse exempts the employee**
  - **Given** the employee has answered civil status and, where asked, the spouse's country of residence, nationality and C permit
  - **When** Lonio decides whether the spouse exempts the employee from source tax
  - **Then** the spouse exempts the employee only if the employee is married or in a registered partnership and the spouse or partner lives in Switzerland and is Swiss or holds a C permit. In every other case (not married for source tax, including separated; spouse living abroad; spouse neither Swiss nor holding a C permit) the spouse does not exempt.
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-d7431e, F-hr_transcript-b6d9b3, F-hr_transcript-f3ce84, D-003, D-005, D-008_

  | Example | Country (home address) | Nationality | Holds a C permit (permanent residence) | Civil status | Spouse's / partner's country of residence | Spouse's / partner's nationality | Spouse / partner holds a C permit | → | Spouse exempts from source tax | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Tomás, Swiss wife in Winterthur (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-d7431e) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Married | Switzerland | Switzerland | – | → | yes | ✅ |
  | Registered partner with Swiss partner (illustrative) | Switzerland | Germany | no | Registered partnership | Switzerland | Switzerland | – | → | yes | ✅ |
  | Spouse with C permit living in Switzerland (illustrative) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Married | Switzerland | Italy | yes | → | yes | ✅ |
  | Spouse without C permit living in Switzerland (illustrative) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Married | Switzerland | Italy | no | → | no | ✅ |
  | Spouse living abroad (illustrative) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Married | Germany | – | – | → | no | ✅ |
  | Separated employee (counts as not married) (illustrative) | Switzerland | Brazil | no | Separated | – | – | – | → | no | ✅ |

## Evidence
- **D-003** (product decision (Product owner)): Civil status offers Single, Married, Divorced, Widowed, Registered partnership and Separated. For source tax, registered partnership counts as married (including the spouse exemption) and separated counts as single, as in the calculator. For the pension fund, provisionally, registered partnership is entered as Married with the partner's date of birth, and separated as Married. Keep OQ-civil-status-partnership open as non-blocking, to be verified by a domain expert.
- **D-005** (product decision (Product owner)): Do not ask for the permit type. Ask non-Swiss employees only whether they hold a C permit (permanent residence), yes or no, and the same for a non-Swiss spouse. No party needs any other permit type. This closes OQ-permit-list. To be verified by a domain expert.
- **D-008** (product decision (Product owner)): Anyone with Swiss citizenship counts as Swiss for source tax, even with another nationality. This closes OQ-dual-nationality. To be verified by a domain expert.
- **F-hr_transcript-b6d9b3** (hr_transcript): Consolidated final rule: an employee is subject to source tax if they live abroad (home address country not Switzerland), regardless of nationality. If they live in Switzerland, they are subject only if they are not Swiss and do not hold a C permit, unless they are married to a spouse who is Swiss or holds a C permit and lives in Switzerland (then not subject).
  - "If you don't have a Swiss passport, you do source tax." — hr_transcript/T-010, Anna
  - "So, not the people with a C permit." — hr_transcript/T-038, Anna
  - "If you're married to someone Swiss, or to someone with a C permit, and that person lives here, then you don't do source tax either." — hr_transcript/T-044, Anna
  - "Those people do source tax because they work here and they don't live here." — hr_transcript/T-048, Anna
- **F-hr_transcript-d7431e** (hr_transcript): An employee married to someone Swiss or to someone with a C permit, where that spouse lives in Switzerland, is not subject to source tax (e.g. Tomás: Brazilian, B permit, Swiss wife, living in Winterthur → no source tax).
  - "If you're married to someone Swiss, or to someone with a C permit, and that person lives here, then you don't do source tax either." — hr_transcript/T-044, Anna
  - "Brazilian, B permit, but his wife is Swiss, and they live in Winterthur. So no source tax for him." — hr_transcript/T-044, Anna
- **F-hr_transcript-f3ce84** (hr_transcript): For married employees, the spouse's nationality, permit (if any) and whether the spouse lives in Switzerland are needed to determine source tax liability.
  - "So you need to know about the spouse as well." — hr_transcript/T-045, Leo
  - "Yes. Their nationality, their permit if they have one, and that they live here." — hr_transcript/T-046, Anna
- **G-01** (product owner guidance): Lonio collects onboarding data from a new employee **once** and reuses it for every receiving party. Asking too little blocks a registration; asking too much collects personal data we must not have (data minimisation). A question must only be asked when at least one receiving party needs the answer for this person.
- **G-10** (product owner guidance): Business wording for values (used in allowed values and examples): countries by English name ("Switzerland", "Germany", "Liechtenstein"), permits as "B permit", "C permit", civil status exactly as the pension fund's list shows it ("Single", "Married", ...), dates as DD.MM.YYYY, yes/no as "yes"/"no".
