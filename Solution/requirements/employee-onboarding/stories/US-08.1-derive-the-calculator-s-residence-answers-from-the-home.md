# US-08.1 – Derive the calculator's residence answers from the home address and ask cross-border questions only of Germany and Liechtenstein residents

Part of [Employee onboarding questionnaire](../feature.md), section [6. Source tax: residence abroad and cross-border questions](../feature.md#SEC-08-cross-border-residence).

**As** new employee, **I want** Lonio to take my country of residence from the home address I already gave, and to ask me cross-border questions only if I live in Germany or Liechtenstein, **so that** I do not answer the same question twice or answer questions that do not apply to me, while Lonio still has everything it needs to compute my source tax tariff code

Priority **P1** – Lonio computes the full tariff code itself (D-001); without the residence answers the tariff code for employees living abroad cannot be computed. HR today does not know up front who must answer which questions (F-hr_transcript-b84498, F-hr_transcript-8595fb). · Status: **inferred** · Sources: F-zh_source_tax_calculator-aedb40, F-zh_source_tax_calculator-4c65ae, F-zh_source_tax_calculator-d62dd4, F-zh_source_tax_calculator-f86c6b, F-zh_source_tax_calculator-31d762, F-zh_source_tax_calculator-e22d17, F-hr_transcript-8595fb, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, G-01, G-08, D-001, D-036, D-041, D-047

Covers (details in [the section](../feature.md#SEC-08-cross-border-residence) and [information.md](../information.md)): Daily return to Germany, Daily return to Liechtenstein, Residence for the tariff calculation

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-08.1.1 Residence for the tariff calculation is derived, not asked**
  - **Given** the employee has entered the country of their home address as it applies on the first working day (step 2 Home address)
  - **When** Lonio determines the residence for the source tax tariff calculation
  - **Then** the calculator's questions 'main residence in Switzerland?' and 'country of residence' are not asked again; the residence is Switzerland for Switzerland, Germany for Germany, Liechtenstein for Liechtenstein, and 'Other country' for any other country. Switzerland and 'Other country' lead to the base tariff without further residence questions. This derivation is inferred from the calculator's options and is covered by the go-live check of the computed tariff code (see OQ-calculator-coverage).
  - _Sources: F-zh_source_tax_calculator-aedb40, F-zh_source_tax_calculator-4c65ae, F-zh_source_tax_calculator-d62dd4, F-zh_source_tax_calculator-f86c6b, G-01, G-08, D-001, D-041, D-009_

  | Example | Country of residence (home address country) | → | Residence for the tariff calculation | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Lives in Switzerland (illustrative) | Switzerland | → | Switzerland | ✅ |
  | Lives in Germany (Konstanz, hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a) (illustrative) | Germany | → | Germany | ✅ |
  | Lives in Liechtenstein (illustrative) | Liechtenstein | → | Liechtenstein | ✅ |
  | Lives in France (illustrative) | France | → | Other country | ✅ |

- **AC-08.1.2 Cross-border questions only for Germany and Liechtenstein residents**
  - **Given** the employee has entered the country of their home address
  - **When** the employee reaches step 5 Cross-border commuting
  - **Then** an employee living in Germany is asked whether they return daily to their main residence in Germany; an employee living in Liechtenstein is asked whether they return daily to their main residence in Liechtenstein; an employee living in Switzerland or any other country is asked no cross-border question and step 5 is skipped. Employees living in Germany or Liechtenstein are always subject to source tax, as everyone living abroad is.
  - _Sources: F-zh_source_tax_calculator-d62dd4, F-zh_source_tax_calculator-f86c6b, F-zh_source_tax_calculator-31d762, F-zh_source_tax_calculator-e22d17, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, D-047_

  | Example | Country of residence (home address country) | → | Daily return to Germany | Daily return to Liechtenstein | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Lives in Germany (illustrative) | Germany | → | asked | not asked | ✅ |
  | Lives in Liechtenstein (illustrative) | Liechtenstein | → | not asked | asked | ✅ |
  | Lives in Switzerland (illustrative) | Switzerland | → | not asked | not asked | ✅ |
  | Lives in France (illustrative) | France | → | not asked | not asked | ✅ |

- **AC-08.1.3 Cross-border answers that no longer apply are discarded**
  - **Given** the employee has answered cross-border questions for Germany or Liechtenstein and then changes the home address country to another country before submitting
  - **When** the employee submits the questionnaire
  - **Then** the cross-border answers that no longer apply are discarded and not recorded; until submission they are kept, so that switching back restores them without retyping
  - _Sources: D-036, D-050, G-01_

## Evidence
- **D-001** (product decision (Product owner)): Lonio computes the full Zurich source tax tariff code (tariff letter including the cross-border letters, child digit and church-tax suffix, e.g. "C1Y", "L0Y") for every employee subject to source tax, from the answers it collects, following the calculator's logic. HR no longer clicks through the calculator.
- **D-009** (product decision (Product owner)): Before go-live a domain expert compares Lonio's computed tariff code with the calculator for the paths not explored (residence block for A/B/H, more than 3 children, H3N/P3N). Special tariffs such as D (second job) are out of scope for onboarding. Keep OQ-calculator-coverage open as a non-blocking go-live condition.
- **D-036** (product decision (Product owner)): Answers to questions that no longer apply after the employee changes an earlier answer are discarded on submission; until then they may be kept so going back and forth loses no input.
- **D-041** (product decision (Product owner)): The residence country and address asked are those that apply on the first working day. Assumption - to be verified by a domain expert; state this assumption in the user story.
- **D-047** (product decision (Product owner)): The questionnaire has exactly these steps, in this order, and feature.md, the flow rule and the stories use the same list. 1 About you (first name, last name, date of birth, first working day); 2 Home address (as on the first working day, canton only in Switzerland); 3 Nationality and C permit (only employees living in Switzerland); 4 Civil status and spouse (civil status is asked of everyone, wherever they live); 5 Cross-border commuting (Liechtenstein and Germany residents subject to source tax); 6 Source tax details (denomination, spouse gainfully employed, has children); 7 Children (one block per child); 8 Permit reminder, summary and submission. There is no separate step for the first working day. Assumption - to be verified by a domain expert; state this assumption in the user story.
- **D-050** (product decision (Product owner)): Build-time values. HR provides the permit reminder wording before go-live. Country of residence and nationality are chosen from the ISO 3166 country list; nationality also offers 'stateless'. The date of birth must make the employee at least 14 and at most 100 years old on the first working day; the first working day must lie within one year after the questionnaire is sent. Answers are saved as the employee goes and they can resume until they submit; submitting needs one confirmation on a summary page. Answers are kept when the employee goes back and changes an earlier answer (until submission), and this is testable. The spouse's residence is asked as 'Does your spouse live in Switzerland? yes/no', not as a country. Assumption - to be verified by a domain expert; state this assumption in the user story.

- **G-01** (product owner guidance): Lonio collects onboarding data from a new employee **once** and reuses it for every receiving party. Asking too little blocks a registration; asking too much collects personal data we must not have (data minimisation). A question must only be asked when at least one receiving party needs the answer for this person.
- **G-08** (product owner guidance): Prefer asking for primary facts and **deriving** values (e.g. ask the first working day, derive the insurance start date) instead of asking the employee for values they cannot know.
