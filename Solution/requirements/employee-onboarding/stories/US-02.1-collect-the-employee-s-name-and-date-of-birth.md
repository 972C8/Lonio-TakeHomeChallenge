# US-02.1 – Collect the employee's name and date of birth

Part of [Employee onboarding questionnaire](../feature.md), section [2. Personal details and home address](../feature.md#SEC-02-personal-details-address).

**As** pension fund, **I want** every new employee to give their first name, last name and date of birth in the first step 'About you' of the onboarding questionnaire, **so that** the registration at Pensionskasse Muster is never blocked by an empty required field (First name, Last name, Date of birth), and HR can identify the employee and address documents to them

Priority **P1** – The pension fund portal rejects a registration when First name, Last name or Date of birth is empty, so without them the registration is blocked. · Status: **inferred** · Sources: F-pension_portal-db702a, F-pension_portal-c688e4, F-pension_portal-88b436, F-pension_portal-967bc9, F-pension_portal-44a27b, F-hr_transcript-3edf89, D-047, D-050, G-03, G-09

Covers (details in [the section](../feature.md#SEC-02-personal-details-address) and [information.md](../information.md)): First name, Last name, Date of birth

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-02.1.1 First and last name are always asked and required**
  - **Given** a new employee fills in step 1 'About you' of the questionnaire (the step also holds the first working day, which another story covers), whatever they answer elsewhere
  - **When** they try to continue without a first name or a last name
  - **Then** they cannot continue until both are filled in. The first and last name are recorded for the pension fund (its required fields 'First name' and 'Last name') and for HR, which uses the name to identify the employee and address documents. HR's need for the name is inferred: HR confirmed 'name, address' but only explained the address.
  - _Sources: F-pension_portal-db702a, F-pension_portal-c688e4, F-pension_portal-44a27b, F-hr_transcript-3edf89, D-047_

  | Example | Country of residence (country of the home address) | → | First name | Last name | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Any employee: name required (illustrative) | Switzerland | → | required | required | ✅ |
  | Employee living abroad: name required too (illustrative) | Germany | → | required | required | ✅ |

- **AC-02.1.2 Date of birth is always asked and required, as DD.MM.YYYY**
  - **Given** a new employee fills in step 1 'About you'
  - **When** they enter their date of birth
  - **Then** the date of birth is required for every employee and is given as a date in the form DD.MM.YYYY (e.g. 14.03.1990). It is recorded for the pension fund, whose registration form requires 'Date of birth' in that form.
  - _Sources: F-pension_portal-88b436, F-pension_portal-967bc9, F-pension_portal-44a27b, G-10_

  | Example | Country of residence (country of the home address) | → | Date of birth | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Any employee: date of birth required (illustrative) | Liechtenstein | → | required | ✅ |

- **AC-02.1.3 Date of birth must be plausible for the first working day**
  - **Given** a new employee has entered a date of birth and a first working day
  - **When** the date of birth means they would be younger than 14 or older than 100 on the first working day
  - **Then** the date of birth is not accepted and the employee is asked to correct it. An employee who is at least 14 and at most 100 years old on the first working day can continue. Assumption to be verified by a domain expert (D-050): these age limits are a product decision, not stated by the pension fund or HR.
  - _Sources: D-050_
- **AC-02.1.4 No further personal identifiers are asked**
  - **Given** a new employee fills in step 1 'About you'
  - **When** they look at the questions in the step
  - **Then** besides the first working day, only first name, last name and date of birth are asked. No social insurance (AHV) number and no badge photo are asked, because no receiving party in scope needs them.
  - _Sources: G-01, G-03, D-047_

## Evidence
- **D-047** (product decision (Product owner)): The questionnaire has exactly these steps, in this order, and feature.md, the flow rule and the stories use the same list. 1 About you (first name, last name, date of birth, first working day); 2 Home address (as on the first working day, canton only in Switzerland); 3 Nationality and C permit (only employees living in Switzerland); 4 Civil status and spouse (civil status is asked of everyone, wherever they live); 5 Cross-border commuting (Liechtenstein and Germany residents subject to source tax); 6 Source tax details (denomination, spouse gainfully employed, has children); 7 Children (one block per child); 8 Permit reminder, summary and submission. There is no separate step for the first working day. Assumption - to be verified by a domain expert; state this assumption in the user story.
- **D-050** (product decision (Product owner)): Build-time values. HR provides the permit reminder wording before go-live. Country of residence and nationality are chosen from the ISO 3166 country list; nationality also offers 'stateless'. The date of birth must make the employee at least 14 and at most 100 years old on the first working day; the first working day must lie within one year after the questionnaire is sent. Answers are saved as the employee goes and they can resume until they submit; submitting needs one confirmation on a summary page. Answers are kept when the employee goes back and changes an earlier answer (until submission), and this is testable. The spouse's residence is asked as 'Does your spouse live in Switzerland? yes/no', not as a country. Assumption - to be verified by a domain expert; state this assumption in the user story.
