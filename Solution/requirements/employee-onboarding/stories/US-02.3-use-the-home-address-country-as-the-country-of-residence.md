# US-02.3 – Use the home address country as the country of residence

Part of [Employee onboarding questionnaire](../feature.md), section [2. Personal details and home address](../feature.md#SEC-02-personal-details-address).

**As** source tax office, **I want** the country of the employee's home address to count as their country of residence, **so that** the questionnaire can tell from the address alone whether the employee lives abroad, and therefore is subject to source tax, without asking for the country of residence a second time

Priority **P1** – HR cannot tell up front who must answer the source tax questions. Residence abroad alone makes an employee subject to source tax, and this decides which later questions are asked. · Status: **inferred** · Sources: F-hr_transcript-ab3cba, F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, F-zh_source_tax_calculator-aedb40, F-zh_source_tax_calculator-4c65ae, G-01, G-08

> ⚠ Open questions affecting this section: [OQ-canton](../open-questions.md#oq-canton)
Covers (details in [the section](../feature.md#SEC-02-personal-details-address) and [information.md](../information.md)): Country (of the home address)

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-02.3.1 Country of residence is not asked separately**
  - **Given** the employee has given the country of their home address in the Home address step
  - **When** a receiving party needs the employee's country of residence, e.g. for the source tax calculator's 'main residence in Switzerland' and 'country of residence' questions
  - **Then** the country of the home address is used as the country of residence, and the employee is not asked for their country of residence again.
  - _Sources: F-zh_source_tax_calculator-aedb40, F-zh_source_tax_calculator-4c65ae, G-01, G-08_
- **AC-02.3.2 Living abroad makes the employee subject to source tax, whatever their nationality**
  - **Given** the employee's home address is outside Switzerland
  - **When** the questionnaire decides whether the employee is subject to source tax (rule subject_to_source_tax)
  - **Then** the employee is subject to source tax, and they are not asked for their nationality, because it does not change the outcome. Employees whose home address is in Switzerland are asked for their nationality. (Whether Liechtenstein residents really count as subject is open, see OQ-liechtenstein.)
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, F-hr_transcript-ab3cba_

  | Example | Country (of the home address) | → | Employee is subject to source tax | Nationality | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Cross-border employee living in Germany (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a, Konstanz) | Germany | → | yes | not asked | ✅ |
  | Employee living in Liechtenstein (illustrative) | Liechtenstein | → | – | not asked | ✅ |
  | Employee living in Switzerland (illustrative) | Switzerland | → | – | asked | ✅ |


## Evidence
- **F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a** (hr_transcript): Employees who work here but live abroad (examples: Konstanz in Germany, Liechtenstein) are always subject to source tax, regardless of nationality. Even a Swiss citizen living abroad (Stefan) is subject.
  - "Those people do source tax because they work here and they don't live here." — hr_transcript/T-048, Anna
  - "And there it doesn't matter what passport you have, by the way. Stefan is actually Swiss, and he still has it." — hr_transcript/T-048, Anna
  - "So someone living abroad and working here always does it?" — hr_transcript/T-049, Leo
  - "Yes." — hr_transcript/T-050, Anna
- **F-hr_transcript-5af8d0** (hr_transcript, inferred): Consolidated final rule. An employee who lives abroad (outside Switzerland) is subject to source tax regardless of nationality. An employee who lives in Switzerland is subject only if they are non-Swiss without a C permit and are not married to a Swiss national or C-permit holder who lives in Switzerland. Note: Anna said 'here', which is read as Switzerland. Her 'I think' hedges remain.
  - "If you don't have a Swiss passport, you do source tax." — hr_transcript/T-010, Anna
  - "So, not the people with a C permit. They're treated like Swiss people for this." — hr_transcript/T-038, Anna
  - "If you're married to someone Swiss, or to someone with a C permit, and that person lives here, then you don't do source tax either." — hr_transcript/T-044, Anna
  - "Those people do source tax because they work here and they don't live here." — hr_transcript/T-048, Anna
  - "Yes." — hr_transcript/T-050, Anna
- **F-hr_transcript-ab3cba** (hr_transcript, inferred): The employee's country of residence (the country of the home address) is needed to decide source tax liability for cross-border employees.
  - "Those people do source tax because they work here and they don't live here." — hr_transcript/T-048, Anna
- **F-zh_source_tax_calculator-4c65ae** (zh_source_tax_calculator): If the main residence is not in Switzerland, the calculator asks for the country of residence.
  - "Wohnsitzstaat" — zh_source_tax_calculator/C-Q17
- **F-zh_source_tax_calculator-aedb40** (zh_source_tax_calculator): The calculator asks whether the main residence is in Switzerland.
  - "Hauptwohnsitz in der Schweiz?" — zh_source_tax_calculator/C-Q16
- **G-01** (product owner guidance): Lonio collects onboarding data from a new employee **once** and reuses it for every receiving party. Asking too little blocks a registration; asking too much collects personal data we must not have (data minimisation). A question must only be asked when at least one receiving party needs the answer for this person.
- **G-08** (product owner guidance): Prefer asking for primary facts and **deriving** values (e.g. ask the first working day, derive the insurance start date) instead of asking the employee for values they cannot know.
