# US-02.3 – Use the home address country as the country of residence

Part of [Employee onboarding questionnaire](../feature.md), section [2. Personal details and home address](../feature.md#SEC-02-personal-details-address).

**As** source tax office, **I want** the country of the employee's home address to serve as their country of residence, without a separate question, **so that** Lonio can tell whether the employee lives in Switzerland or abroad. That decides who is subject to source tax, which follow-up questions apply (nationality, canton, the cross-border questions for Germany and Liechtenstein), and the tariff code Lonio computes

Priority **P1** – Employees living abroad are always subject to source tax. Without the country of residence neither source tax liability nor the tariff code can be determined, and HR says it never knows up front who has to answer the source tax questions. · Sources: F-hr_transcript-ab3cba, F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, F-hr_transcript-b84498, F-hr_transcript-c0fe95, F-zh_source_tax_calculator-aedb40, F-zh_source_tax_calculator-4c65ae, F-zh_source_tax_calculator-d62dd4, G-01, D-001, D-002

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-02.3.1 The country of residence is not asked separately**
  - **Given** the employee has entered the country of their home address
  - **When** the questionnaire needs the employee's country of residence (for source tax or for the pension fund's canton)
  - **Then** it uses the country of the home address and does not ask a separate country-of-residence question
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-ab3cba, G-01_
- **AC-02.3.2 Employees living abroad are subject to source tax whatever their nationality**
  - **Given** the country of the home address is not Switzerland
  - **When** Lonio decides whether the employee is subject to source tax (rule subject_to_source_tax, owned by the source tax section)
  - **Then** the employee is subject to source tax, and their nationality is not asked. This includes Swiss citizens living abroad (e.g. Stefan) and Liechtenstein residents. Liechtenstein residents are asked the source tax questions, although their result may be 'in principle no source tax liability' (D-007)
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, D-007, D-038_

  | Example | Country of residence (country of the home address) | → | Subject to source tax | Nationality | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Employee living in Konstanz, Germany (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a) | Germany | → | yes | not asked | ✅ |
  | Employee living in Liechtenstein (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a) | Liechtenstein | → | yes | not asked | ✅ |
  | Employee living in another country (illustrative) | France | → | yes | not asked | ✅ |

- **AC-02.3.3 Employees living in Switzerland get the residence-dependent questions**
  - **Given** the country of the home address is Switzerland
  - **When** the employee continues the questionnaire
  - **Then** the employee is asked for the canton (see US-02.4) and for their nationality, which decides source tax liability for residents (owned by another section). The cross-border questions for Germany and Liechtenstein are not asked
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, D-002, F-zh_source_tax_calculator-aedb40_

  | Example | Country of residence (country of the home address) | → | Canton | Nationality | Returns daily to the home in Germany | Returns daily to the home in Liechtenstein | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Tomás, lives in Winterthur (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-b90bd9) | Switzerland | → | asked | asked | not asked | not asked | ✅ |

- **AC-02.3.4 Germany and Liechtenstein lead to their cross-border questions**
  - **Given** the country of the home address is Germany or Liechtenstein
  - **When** the employee continues the questionnaire
  - **Then** the employee is asked that country's cross-border questions, starting with whether they return home daily (owned by the source tax section). Employees living in any other country abroad are asked neither set. This follows the calculator's residence options Germany, Liechtenstein and other country
  - _Sources: F-zh_source_tax_calculator-4c65ae, F-zh_source_tax_calculator-d62dd4, D-001_

  | Example | Country of residence (country of the home address) | → | Returns daily to the home in Germany | Returns daily to the home in Liechtenstein | Canton | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Employee living in Konstanz, Germany (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a) | Germany | → | asked | not asked | not asked | ✅ |
  | Employee living in Liechtenstein (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a) | Liechtenstein | → | not asked | asked | not asked | ✅ |
  | Employee living in another country (illustrative) | France | → | not asked | not asked | not asked | ✅ |


## Evidence
- **D-001** (product decision (Product owner)): Lonio computes the full Zurich source tax tariff code (tariff letter including the cross-border letters, child digit and church-tax suffix, e.g. "C1Y", "L0Y") for every employee subject to source tax, from the answers it collects, following the calculator's logic. HR no longer clicks through the calculator.
- **D-002** (product decision (Product owner)): Ask for the canton (pension fund 'Canton' field) only from employees living in Switzerland. Provisional - keep OQ-canton-abroad open as non-blocking, to be verified by a domain expert and Pensionskasse Muster (what to enter for employees living abroad, and whether 'Canton' means residence or work).
- **D-007** (product decision (Product owner)): Liechtenstein residents are asked the source tax questions (HR's rule decides who is asked). The result follows the calculator - with daily return or at most 45 non-return days, Lonio shows 'in principle no source tax liability, subject to the double taxation agreement' instead of a tariff code, flagged for HR; with more than 45 non-return days, the normal tariff code. This closes OQ-liechtenstein-conflict. To be verified by a domain expert.
- **D-038** (product decision (Product owner)): Employees living abroad get the permit reminder whatever their nationality, because nationality is not asked of them (data minimisation, G-01). HR words the reminder so that a Swiss citizen living abroad, such as Stefan, can see that it does not apply to them. Asking everyone 'Swiss citizen yes/no' only for the reminder is not worth the extra question.
- **F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a** (hr_transcript): Employees who work here but live abroad (examples: Konstanz in Germany, Liechtenstein) are always subject to source tax, regardless of nationality. Even a Swiss citizen living abroad (Stefan) is subject.
  - "Those people do source tax because they work here and they don't live here." — hr_transcript/T-048, Anna
  - "And there it doesn't matter what passport you have, by the way. Stefan is actually Swiss, and he still has it." — hr_transcript/T-048, Anna
  - "So someone living abroad and working here always does it?" — hr_transcript/T-049, Leo
  - "Yes." — hr_transcript/T-050, Anna
- **F-hr_transcript-5af8d0** (hr_transcript): Consolidated final rule. An employee who lives abroad (outside Switzerland) is subject to source tax regardless of nationality. An employee who lives in Switzerland is subject only if they are non-Swiss without a C permit and are not married to a Swiss national or C-permit holder who lives in Switzerland. Note: Anna said 'here', which is read as Switzerland. Her 'I think' hedges remain.
  - "If you don't have a Swiss passport, you do source tax." — hr_transcript/T-010, Anna
  - "So, not the people with a C permit. They're treated like Swiss people for this." — hr_transcript/T-038, Anna
  - "If you're married to someone Swiss, or to someone with a C permit, and that person lives here, then you don't do source tax either." — hr_transcript/T-044, Anna
  - "Those people do source tax because they work here and they don't live here." — hr_transcript/T-048, Anna
  - "Yes." — hr_transcript/T-050, Anna
- **F-hr_transcript-ab3cba** (hr_transcript): The employee's country of residence (the country of the home address) is needed to decide source tax liability for cross-border employees.

- **G-01** (product owner guidance): Lonio collects onboarding data from a new employee **once** and reuses it for every receiving party. Asking too little blocks a registration; asking too much collects personal data we must not have (data minimisation). A question must only be asked when at least one receiving party needs the answer for this person.
