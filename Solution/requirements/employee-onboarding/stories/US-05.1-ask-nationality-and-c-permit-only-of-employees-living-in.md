# US-05.1 – Ask nationality and C permit only of employees living in Switzerland

Part of [Employee onboarding questionnaire](../feature.md), section [5. Who is subject to source tax](../feature.md#SEC-05-source-tax-liability).

**As** HR, **I want** Lonio to ask an employee who lives in Switzerland for their nationality and, if they are not Swiss, only whether they hold a C permit, **so that** I can tell whether the employee is subject to source tax without collecting nationality or permit data from people for whom it changes nothing (employees living abroad are always subject to source tax)

Priority **P1** – Nationality and C permit decide source tax liability for everyone living in Switzerland; without them HR cannot tell who has to answer the source tax questions (F-hr_transcript-b84498). · Status: **decision** · Sources: F-hr_transcript-35fae3, F-hr_transcript-845204, F-hr_transcript-c9d9b9, F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, G-01, D-005, D-008, D-036, D-038, D-047, D-050

Covers (details in [the section](../feature.md#SEC-05-source-tax-liability) and [information.md](../information.md)): Nationality, Holds a C permit, Subject to source tax

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-05.1.1 Nationality is required only from employees living in Switzerland**
  - **Given** the employee has entered the country of their home address in step 2 (the address that applies on the first working day)
  - **When** the employee reaches step 3 'Nationality and C permit'
  - **Then** if the home address is in Switzerland, the nationality is asked and required; if the home address is in any other country, the nationality is not asked, because an employee living abroad is subject to source tax whatever their passport. The nationality is recorded for HR to decide source tax liability and whether the permit reminder applies.
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-35fae3, F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, G-01, D-038, D-047_

  | Example | Country of residence (home address country) | → | Nationality | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | Maria, Portuguese living in Switzerland (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-845204) | Switzerland | → | required | ✅ |
  | Employee living in Konstanz (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a) | Germany | → | not asked | ✅ |
  | Employee living in Liechtenstein (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a) | Liechtenstein | → | not asked | ✅ |
  | Employee living in France (illustrative) | France | → | not asked | ✅ |

- **AC-05.1.2 Allowed nationality answers and dual citizens**
  - **Given** the nationality is asked
  - **When** the employee chooses their nationality
  - **Then** the employee chooses one country from the ISO 3166 country list (English names) or 'stateless'. Anyone with Swiss citizenship chooses Switzerland, even if they also hold another nationality, and counts as Swiss for source tax. Assumption (D-008, D-050) - to be verified by a domain expert.
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-35fae3, D-008, D-050_
- **AC-05.1.3 C permit question only for non-Swiss employees living in Switzerland**
  - **Given** the employee lives in Switzerland and has given their nationality
  - **When** the nationality is not Switzerland
  - **Then** Lonio asks 'Do you hold a C permit (permanent residence)?' with the answers yes or no, and an answer is required; Swiss employees and employees living abroad are not asked. The answer is recorded for HR to decide source tax liability (a C-permit holder is treated like a Swiss person).
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-845204, F-hr_transcript-c9d9b9, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, D-005_

  | Example | Country of residence (home address country) | Nationality | → | Holds a C permit | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Maria, Portuguese, C permit (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-845204) | Switzerland | Portugal | → | required | ✅ |
  | Tomás, Brazilian living in Winterthur (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-b90bd9) | Switzerland | Brazil | → | required | ✅ |
  | Swiss citizen living in Switzerland (illustrative) | Switzerland | Switzerland | → | not asked | ✅ |
  | Stateless employee living in Switzerland (illustrative) | Switzerland | stateless | → | required | ✅ |
  | Employee living in Konstanz (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a) | Germany | – | → | not asked | ✅ |

- **AC-05.1.4 No other permit type is asked**
  - **Given** a non-Swiss employee living in Switzerland
  - **When** the employee answers step 3
  - **Then** Lonio asks only whether they hold a C permit and never asks which permit type they hold (for example a B permit), because no receiving party needs any other permit type. Assumption (D-005) - to be verified by a domain expert.
  - _Sources: D-005, G-01, F-hr_transcript-c9d9b9_
- **AC-05.1.5 Nationality and C permit answers are discarded when the employee moves the home address abroad**
  - **Given** the employee entered a home address in Switzerland, answered nationality and C permit, then went back and changed the home address country to another country
  - **When** the employee submits the questionnaire
  - **Then** the nationality and C permit answers are no longer asked and are not kept; until submission they may be kept so that switching back loses no input.
  - _Sources: D-036, D-050, G-01_

  | Example | Country of residence (home address country) | Nationality | Holds a C permit | → | Nationality | Holds a C permit | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Employee changes the home address from Switzerland to Germany (illustrative) | Germany | Italy | no | → | not asked | not asked | ✅ |


## Evidence
- **D-005** (product decision (Product owner)): Do not ask for the permit type. Ask non-Swiss employees only whether they hold a C permit (permanent residence), yes or no, and the same for a non-Swiss spouse. No party needs any other permit type. This closes OQ-permit-list. To be verified by a domain expert.
- **D-008** (product decision (Product owner)): Anyone with Swiss citizenship counts as Swiss for source tax, even with another nationality. This closes OQ-dual-nationality. To be verified by a domain expert.
- **D-036** (product decision (Product owner)): Answers to questions that no longer apply after the employee changes an earlier answer are discarded on submission; until then they may be kept so going back and forth loses no input.
- **D-038** (product decision (Product owner)): Employees living abroad get the permit reminder whatever their nationality, because nationality is not asked of them (data minimisation, G-01). HR words the reminder so that a Swiss citizen living abroad, such as Stefan, can see that it does not apply to them. Asking everyone 'Swiss citizen yes/no' only for the reminder is not worth the extra question.
- **D-047** (product decision (Product owner)): The questionnaire has exactly these steps, in this order, and feature.md, the flow rule and the stories use the same list. 1 About you (first name, last name, date of birth, first working day); 2 Home address (as on the first working day, canton only in Switzerland); 3 Nationality and C permit (only employees living in Switzerland); 4 Civil status and spouse (civil status is asked of everyone, wherever they live); 5 Cross-border commuting (Liechtenstein and Germany residents subject to source tax); 6 Source tax details (denomination, spouse gainfully employed, has children); 7 Children (one block per child); 8 Permit reminder, summary and submission. There is no separate step for the first working day. Assumption - to be verified by a domain expert; state this assumption in the user story.
- **D-050** (product decision (Product owner)): Build-time values. HR provides the permit reminder wording before go-live. Country of residence and nationality are chosen from the ISO 3166 country list; nationality also offers 'stateless'. The date of birth must make the employee at least 14 and at most 100 years old on the first working day; the first working day must lie within one year after the questionnaire is sent. Answers are saved as the employee goes and they can resume until they submit; submitting needs one confirmation on a summary page. Answers are kept when the employee goes back and changes an earlier answer (until submission), and this is testable. The spouse's residence is asked as 'Does your spouse live in Switzerland? yes/no', not as a country. Assumption - to be verified by a domain expert; state this assumption in the user story.
