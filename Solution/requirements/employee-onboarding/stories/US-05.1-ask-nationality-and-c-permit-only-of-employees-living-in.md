# US-05.1 – Ask nationality and C permit only of employees living in Switzerland

Part of [Employee onboarding questionnaire](../feature.md), section [5. Who is subject to source tax](../feature.md#SEC-05-source-tax-liability).

**As** new employee, **I want** to be asked my nationality and, if I am not Swiss, only whether I hold a C permit - and only when I live in Switzerland, **so that** Lonio can decide whether I am subject to source tax without collecting nationality or permit data that no receiving party needs for me (data minimisation)

Priority **P1** – Source tax liability cannot be decided for residents of Switzerland without these answers; HR names nationality and the C permit as the deciding facts (F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, F-hr_transcript-845204). · Status: **decision** · Sources: F-hr_transcript-35fae3, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, F-hr_transcript-845204, F-hr_transcript-c9d9b9, F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a, G-01, D-005, D-008, D-038, D-047, D-050

Covers (details in [the section](../feature.md#SEC-05-source-tax-liability) and [information.md](../information.md)): Nationality, Holds a C permit

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-05.1.1 Nationality is asked and required only of employees living in Switzerland**
  - **Given** the employee has entered the country of their home address as it applies on the first working day (step 2 'Home address')
  - **When** the employee reaches step 3 'Nationality and C permit'
  - **Then** the nationality is asked and required if the country of residence is Switzerland; it is not asked of employees living abroad, who are subject to source tax whatever their nationality (D-038). Nationality is recorded for HR to decide source tax liability and whether the permit reminder applies.
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-35fae3, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a, G-01, D-038, D-047_

  | Example | Country of residence (country of the home address) | Nationality | → | Nationality | Holds a C permit | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Maria (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-845204) | Switzerland | Portugal | → | required | – | ✅ |
  | Stefan, Swiss citizen living abroad (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a) (illustrative) | Germany | – | → | not asked | not asked | ✅ |
  | Employee living in Liechtenstein (illustrative) | Liechtenstein | – | → | not asked | – | ✅ |

- **AC-05.1.2 Nationality answer values; Swiss citizenship counts as Swiss**
  - **Given** the nationality question is asked
  - **When** the employee answers it
  - **Then** the employee chooses a country from the country list by English name, or 'stateless'. Anyone holding Swiss citizenship, also together with another nationality, answers Switzerland and counts as Swiss. Assumption - to be verified by a domain expert: the country list with 'stateless' (D-050) and dual nationals counting as Swiss (D-008).
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-35fae3, D-008, D-050, G-10_
- **AC-05.1.3 C permit is asked only of non-Swiss employees living in Switzerland**
  - **Given** the employee lives in Switzerland and has answered the nationality question
  - **When** the nationality is not Switzerland
  - **Then** the question 'Do you hold a C permit (permanent residence)?' is asked and required, with the answers 'yes' or 'no'; it is not asked of Swiss nationals or of employees living abroad. No other permit type is asked (assumption D-005, to be verified by a domain expert). The answer is recorded for HR to decide source tax liability.
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-845204, F-hr_transcript-c9d9b9, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, D-005, D-047_

  | Example | Country of residence (country of the home address) | Nationality | → | Holds a C permit | Checked |

  | Maria (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-845204) | Switzerland | Portugal | → | required | ✅ |
  | Tomás (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-b90bd9) | Switzerland | Brazil | → | required | ✅ |

  | Employee living in Germany (illustrative) | Germany | – | → | not asked | ✅ |


## Evidence
- **D-005** (product decision (Product owner)): Do not ask for the permit type. Ask non-Swiss employees only whether they hold a C permit (permanent residence), yes or no, and the same for a non-Swiss spouse. No party needs any other permit type. This closes OQ-permit-list. To be verified by a domain expert.
- **D-008** (product decision (Product owner)): Anyone with Swiss citizenship counts as Swiss for source tax, even with another nationality. This closes OQ-dual-nationality. To be verified by a domain expert.
- **D-038** (product decision (Product owner)): Employees living abroad get the permit reminder whatever their nationality, because nationality is not asked of them (data minimisation, G-01). HR words the reminder so that a Swiss citizen living abroad, such as Stefan, can see that it does not apply to them. Asking everyone 'Swiss citizen yes/no' only for the reminder is not worth the extra question.
- **D-047** (product decision (Product owner)): The questionnaire has exactly these steps, in this order, and feature.md, the flow rule and the stories use the same list. 1 About you (first name, last name, date of birth, first working day); 2 Home address (as on the first working day, canton only in Switzerland); 3 Nationality and C permit (only employees living in Switzerland); 4 Civil status and spouse (civil status is asked of everyone, wherever they live); 5 Cross-border commuting (Liechtenstein and Germany residents subject to source tax); 6 Source tax details (denomination, spouse gainfully employed, has children); 7 Children (one block per child); 8 Permit reminder, summary and submission. There is no separate step for the first working day. Assumption - to be verified by a domain expert; state this assumption in the user story.
- **D-050** (product decision (Product owner)): Build-time values. HR provides the permit reminder wording before go-live. Country of residence and nationality are chosen from the ISO 3166 country list; nationality also offers 'stateless'. The date of birth must make the employee at least 14 and at most 100 years old on the first working day; the first working day must lie within one year after the questionnaire is sent. Answers are saved as the employee goes and they can resume until they submit; submitting needs one confirmation on a summary page. Answers are kept when the employee goes back and changes an earlier answer (until submission), and this is testable. The spouse's residence is asked as 'Does your spouse live in Switzerland? yes/no', not as a country. Assumption - to be verified by a domain expert; state this assumption in the user story.

- **G-01** (product owner guidance): Lonio collects onboarding data from a new employee **once** and reuses it for every receiving party. Asking too little blocks a registration; asking too much collects personal data we must not have (data minimisation). A question must only be asked when at least one receiving party needs the answer for this person.
- **G-10** (product owner guidance): Business wording for values (used in allowed values and examples): countries by English name ("Switzerland", "Germany", "Liechtenstein"), permits as "B permit", "C permit", civil status exactly as the pension fund's list shows it ("Single", "Married", ...), dates as DD.MM.YYYY, yes/no as "yes"/"no".
