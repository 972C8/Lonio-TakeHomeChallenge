# US-05.2 – Ask the spouse's nationality, permit and residence only when they can exempt the employee

Part of [Employee onboarding questionnaire](../feature.md), section [5. Who is subject to source tax](../feature.md#SEC-05-source-tax-liability).

**As** HR, **I want** the questionnaire to ask a married employee for the spouse's nationality, permit and country of residence, but only when the employee would otherwise be subject to source tax, **so that** I no longer have to check the spouse exemption by hand and chase the spouse details by email, and no spouse data is collected when it cannot change the outcome

Priority **P1** – HR checks the spouse exemption by hand today and asks for the spouse details by email, which 'takes forever'. Getting it wrong sends the source tax questions to the wrong people. · Status: **inferred** · Sources: F-hr_transcript-f3ce84, F-hr_transcript-76eacc, F-hr_transcript-e5337d, F-hr_transcript-b90bd9, F-hr_transcript-54d011, F-hr_transcript-a45422, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, G-01, G-10

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-05.2.1 Spouse's nationality is asked only of married employees who would otherwise be subject**
  - **Given** the employee has given their home country, nationality, permit (if asked) and civil status (asked in the family step)
  - **When** the employee is "Married", lives in Switzerland, is not Swiss and does not hold a C permit
  - **Then** the spouse's nationality is asked and required. It is not asked of employees who are not married, who live abroad, who are Swiss or who hold a C permit, because the spouse cannot change their outcome.
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-f3ce84, F-hr_transcript-b90bd9, F-hr_transcript-54d011, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, G-01_

  | Example | Country (of the home address) | Nationality | Permit type | Civil status | → | Spouse's nationality | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Tomás, Brazilian, B permit, married (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-b90bd9) | Switzerland | Brazil | B permit | Married | → | required | ✅ |
  | Single employee with a B permit (illustrative) | Switzerland | India | B permit | Single | → | not asked | ✅ |
  | Married employee with a C permit (illustrative) | Switzerland | Portugal | C permit | Married | → | not asked | ✅ |
  | Married Swiss employee living in Switzerland (illustrative) | Switzerland | Switzerland | – | Married | → | not asked | ✅ |
  | Married employee living in Germany (illustrative) | Germany | – | – | Married | → | not asked | ✅ |

- **AC-05.2.2 Spouse's permit is asked only if the spouse is not Swiss**
  - **Given** the spouse's nationality was asked (see AC-05.2.1)
  - **When** the spouse's nationality is not Switzerland
  - **Then** the spouse's Swiss permit is asked. If the spouse is Swiss, the permit is not asked. A spouse may have no permit at all (HR: 'their permit if they have one'); how the employee says so is open (see OQ-permit-list).
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-76eacc, F-hr_transcript-b90bd9, F-hr_transcript-54d011, G-01_

  | Example | Country (of the home address) | Nationality | Permit type | Civil status | Spouse's nationality | → | Spouse's permit type | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Tomás, wife is Swiss (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-b90bd9) | Switzerland | Brazil | B permit | Married | Switzerland | → | not asked | ✅ |
  | Spouse is Italian (illustrative) | Switzerland | Brazil | B permit | Married | Italy | → | asked | ✅ |

- **AC-05.2.3 Spouse's residence is asked only if the spouse is Swiss or holds a C permit**
  - **Given** the spouse's nationality was asked (see AC-05.2.1)
  - **When** the spouse is Swiss or holds a C permit
  - **Then** the spouse's country of residence is asked and required, because the exemption applies only if that spouse lives in Switzerland. If the spouse is neither Swiss nor a C-permit holder, the spouse's residence is not asked. Whether it is asked as a country or as 'Does your spouse live in Switzerland? yes/no' is open (see OQ-spouse-residence-form).
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-e5337d, F-hr_transcript-b90bd9, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, G-01_

  | Example | Country (of the home address) | Nationality | Permit type | Civil status | Spouse's nationality | Spouse's permit type | → | Spouse's country of residence | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Tomás, wife is Swiss (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-b90bd9) | Switzerland | Brazil | B permit | Married | Switzerland | – | → | required | ✅ |
  | Spouse is Italian with a C permit (illustrative) | Switzerland | Brazil | B permit | Married | Italy | C permit | → | asked | ✅ |
  | Spouse is Italian with a B permit (illustrative) | Switzerland | Brazil | B permit | Married | Italy | B permit | → | not asked | ✅ |

- **AC-05.2.4 Answer form and use of the spouse details**
  - **Given** spouse questions are asked
  - **When** the employee answers them
  - **Then** nationality and country of residence are given as countries by their English name and the permit as e.g. "B permit" or "C permit". The answers are used only to decide whether the employee is subject to source tax, so HR does not need to ask for spouse details by email. Whether employees in a registered partnership get these questions is open (see OQ-registered-partnership).
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-f3ce84, F-hr_transcript-76eacc, F-hr_transcript-e5337d, F-hr_transcript-a45422, G-10_

- **G-01** (product owner guidance): Lonio collects onboarding data from a new employee **once** and reuses it for every receiving party. Asking too little blocks a registration; asking too much collects personal data we must not have (data minimisation). A question must only be asked when at least one receiving party needs the answer for this person.
- **G-10** (product owner guidance): Business wording for values (used in allowed values and examples): countries by English name ("Switzerland", "Germany", "Liechtenstein"), permits as "B permit", "C permit", civil status exactly as the pension fund's list shows it ("Single", "Married", ...), dates as DD.MM.YYYY, yes/no as "yes"/"no".
