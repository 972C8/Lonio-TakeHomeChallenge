# US-01.2 – Ask the steps in an order that avoids unneeded questions

Part of [Employee onboarding questionnaire](../feature.md), section [1. Questionnaire flow overview](../feature.md#SEC-01-questionnaire-flow).

**As** new employee, **I want** to go through the questionnaire in a fixed order of steps, where earlier answers decide which later questions I see, **so that** I am never asked a question whose answer nobody needs for me, such as my nationality when I live abroad or my spouse's details when I am not married

Priority **P2** – Enables data minimisation and avoids annoying employees with irrelevant questions; it structures the questionnaire rather than unblocking a registration on its own. · Status: **inferred** · Sources: G-01, G-08, G-09, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a, F-hr_transcript-54d011, F-hr_transcript-b84498

Covers (details in [the section](../feature.md#SEC-01-questionnaire-flow) and [information.md](../information.md)): Questionnaire flow overview

## Acceptance criteria

- **AC-01.2.1 The steps follow a fixed order in English**
  - **Given** any new employee of Muster AG
  - **When** they open the onboarding questionnaire
  - **Then** the questionnaire is in English and the steps come in this order: 1 Personal details (first name, last name, date of birth); 2 Home address (street, house number, postcode, town, country, and the canton if the employee lives in Switzerland); 3 Employment start (the first working day); 4 Nationality and permit; 5 Family (civil status and, where they apply, spouse questions); 6 Source tax. Steps 4 and 6 and the spouse questions in step 5 appear only under the conditions in the other criteria of this story and in US-01.1's rule
  - _Sources: G-09, G-01, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0_
- **AC-01.2.2 Home address comes before nationality; employees living abroad skip step 4**
  - **Given** the employee has entered their home address in step 2
  - **When** the country of the home address is not Switzerland
  - **Then** step 4 Nationality and permit is skipped entirely, because employees living abroad are subject to source tax whatever their nationality; if the country is Switzerland, the nationality is asked, and the permit only if the employee is not Swiss (for employees with several nationalities see OQ-dual-nationality)
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, F-hr_transcript-c9d9b9, G-01_

  | Example | Country (of the home address) | Nationality | → | Nationality | Permit type | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Employee living in Germany (illustrative) | Germany | – | → | not asked | not asked | ✅ |
  | Swiss employee living in Switzerland (illustrative) | Switzerland | Switzerland | → | asked | not asked | ✅ |
  | Maria, Portuguese living in Switzerland (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-845204) | Switzerland | Portugal | → | asked | asked | ✅ |

- **AC-01.2.3 Civil status comes before the spouse questions**
  - **Given** the employee is in step 5 Family
  - **When** they have answered their civil status
  - **Then** the spouse's nationality, permit and residence are asked only if the employee is married and would otherwise be subject to source tax (lives in Switzerland, is not Swiss and has no C permit); the spouse's residence is asked only if the spouse is Swiss or holds a C permit; otherwise none of these spouse questions appear
  - _Sources: F-hr_transcript-54d011, F-hr_transcript-b90bd9, F-hr_transcript-5af8d0, G-01_

  | Example | Country (of the home address) | Nationality | Permit type | Civil status | Spouse's nationality | → | Spouse's nationality | Spouse's permit type | Spouse's country of residence | Checked |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Tomás, Brazilian with a B permit, married to a Swiss wife (hr_transcript/F-hr_transcript-b90bd9) | Switzerland | Brazil | B permit | Married | Switzerland | → | asked | not asked | asked | ✅ |
  | Single B-permit employee living in Switzerland (illustrative) | Switzerland | Brazil | B permit | Single | – | → | not asked | not asked | not asked | ✅ |
  | Married Swiss employee living in Switzerland (illustrative) | Switzerland | Switzerland | – | Married | – | → | not asked | – | not asked | ✅ |
  | Married employee living in Germany (illustrative) | Germany | – | – | Married | – | → | not asked | not asked | – | ✅ |

- **AC-01.2.4 The employee confirms the first working day; the insurance start date is derived**
  - **Given** the employee is in step 3 Employment start
  - **When** the step is shown
  - **Then** the employee is asked to confirm their first working day and is not asked for the pension insurance start date; that date is derived from the first working day (rule insurance_start_date)
  - _Sources: G-08_

## Evidence
- **F-hr_transcript-3f2c2a** (hr_transcript): Employees who work here but live abroad (examples: Konstanz in Germany, Liechtenstein) are always subject to source tax, regardless of nationality. Even a Swiss citizen living abroad (Stefan) is subject.
  - "Those people do source tax because they work here and they don't live here." — hr_transcript/T-048, Anna
  - "And there it doesn't matter what passport you have, by the way. Stefan is actually Swiss, and he still has it." — hr_transcript/T-048, Anna
  - "So someone living abroad and working here always does it?" — hr_transcript/T-049, Leo
  - "Yes." — hr_transcript/T-050, Anna
- **F-hr_transcript-54d011** (hr_transcript, inferred): The spouse's nationality, permit and residence only matter for source tax when the employee is married. They are presumably only needed when the employee would otherwise be subject to source tax (non-Swiss, no C permit, living in Switzerland).
  - "it also changes who has to do it at all." — hr_transcript/T-044, Anna
  - "then you don't do source tax either." — hr_transcript/T-044, Anna
- **F-hr_transcript-5af8d0** (hr_transcript, inferred): Consolidated final rule. An employee who lives abroad (outside Switzerland) is subject to source tax regardless of nationality. An employee who lives in Switzerland is subject only if they are non-Swiss without a C permit and are not married to a Swiss national or C-permit holder who lives in Switzerland. Note: Anna said 'here', which is read as Switzerland. Her 'I think' hedges remain.
  - "If you don't have a Swiss passport, you do source tax." — hr_transcript/T-010, Anna
  - "So, not the people with a C permit. They're treated like Swiss people for this." — hr_transcript/T-038, Anna
  - "If you're married to someone Swiss, or to someone with a C permit, and that person lives here, then you don't do source tax either." — hr_transcript/T-044, Anna
  - "Those people do source tax because they work here and they don't live here." — hr_transcript/T-048, Anna
  - "Yes." — hr_transcript/T-050, Anna
- **F-hr_transcript-b84498** (hr_transcript, inferred): Source tax questions should only be asked of employees who are subject to source tax, not of everybody.
  - "Because I never know up front who has to answer those questions." — hr_transcript/T-008, Anna
  - "So who actually needs to answer them?" — hr_transcript/T-009, Leo
- **F-hr_transcript-b90bd9** (hr_transcript): An employee married to a Swiss person, or to someone with a C permit, is not subject to source tax if that spouse lives 'here'. Example: Tomás, Brazilian with a B permit, has a Swiss wife and they live in Winterthur, so he has no source tax.
  - "If you're married to someone Swiss, or to someone with a C permit, and that person lives here, then you don't do source tax either." — hr_transcript/T-044, Anna
  - "Tomás in logistics, for example. Brazilian, B permit, but his wife is Swiss, and they live in Winterthur. So no source tax for him." — hr_transcript/T-044, Anna
- **F-hr_transcript-c9d9b9** (hr_transcript, inferred): The permit type of non-Swiss employees is needed to decide source tax liability (a C permit exempts them). The permit type is only relevant for non-Swiss employees.
  - "So, not the people with a C permit." — hr_transcript/T-038, Anna
- **G-01** (product owner guidance): Lonio collects onboarding data from a new employee **once** and reuses it for every receiving party. Asking too little blocks a registration; asking too much collects personal data we must not have (data minimisation). A question must only be asked when at least one receiving party needs the answer for this person.
- **G-08** (product owner guidance): Prefer asking for primary facts and **deriving** values (e.g. ask the first working day, derive the insurance start date) instead of asking the employee for values they cannot know.
- **G-09** (product owner guidance): Questionnaire language: English.
