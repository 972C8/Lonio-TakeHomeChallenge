# Information catalogue

Every piece of information the process needs, **asked once** and reused by
every stakeholder. This view answers, for each piece: what, when we ask, when
it is required, what it depends on, and who needs it.

## Derived results (never asked)

- **Insurance start date** – The pension fund insurance start date is derived from the latest first working day: if the first working day is the 1st of a month, insurance starts that day; otherwise on the 1st of the following month (e.g. 15.03.2026 → 01.04.2026). It is always the 1st of a month and is derived again whenever the first working day changes. ([First working day and insurance start date](feature.md#SEC-02-start-date-insurance))
- **Civil status for the pension fund** – The civil status entered at the pension fund is taken from the employee's civil status; registered partnership and separated are provisionally entered as Married (with the spouse's/partner's date of birth). ([Civil status and spouse for the pension fund](feature.md#SEC-03-civil-status-spouse))
- **Civil status for source tax** – For source tax, married and registered partnership count as 'married or registered partnership'; single, divorced, separated and widowed count as 'single, divorced, separated or widowed', as in the calculator. ([Civil status and spouse for the pension fund](feature.md#SEC-03-civil-status-spouse))
- **Spouse exemption from source tax** – An employee who is married or in a registered partnership with a spouse/partner who is Swiss or holds a C permit and lives in Switzerland is exempt from source tax. ([Source tax liability check](feature.md#SEC-04-source-tax-liability))
- **Subject to source tax** – An employee living abroad is always subject to source tax, whatever their nationality (also Swiss citizens). An employee living in Switzerland is subject only if not Swiss, without a C permit and without the spouse exemption. Only employees subject to source tax get the source tax questions. ([Source tax liability check](feature.md#SEC-04-source-tax-liability))
- **Residence for source tax** – The calculator's residence answers are derived from the country of the home address: Switzerland (main residence in Switzerland), Germany, Liechtenstein, or any other country ('other country', which leaves the base tariff unchanged). ([Employees living abroad (Germany, Liechtenstein)](feature.md#SEC-07-living-abroad))
- **Church-tax suffix** – The tariff code ends in Y for Roman Catholic, Christian Catholic or Evangelical Reformed, and in N for none or other. ([Source tax tariff basics and computed result](feature.md#SEC-05-tariff-basics))
- **Base tariff letter for married employees** – For married employees and registered partners subject to source tax: tariff letter C if the spouse/partner works or receives replacement income, otherwise B. ([Source tax tariff basics and computed result](feature.md#SEC-05-tariff-basics))
- **How a child counts for the tariff** – For each child, the answers decide whether the child counts for the tariff digit, and - for single, divorced, separated or widowed employees - whether it counts as an H child (lives in the household and the employee has sole custody or is the main contributor). Cases the calculator exploration did not cover are 'not determined' until verified (OQ-child-paths-undetermined). The expression evaluates one child; the rule applies to each child. ([Children for the source tax tariff](feature.md#SEC-06-children))
- **Tariff child digit** – The tariff digit is the number of children who count (including H children); 0 without children. The expression covers a single child; with several children the counts are added up. ([Children for the source tax tariff](feature.md#SEC-06-children))
- **Single-parent tariff H** – For a single, divorced, separated or widowed employee, any child counted as H child gives the H tariff overall (e.g. one A-counting and one H child → H2N). ([Children for the source tax tariff](feature.md#SEC-06-children))
- **Cross-border tariff applies** – For employees living in Germany, the cross-border tariff applies with daily return and a certificate of residence ('not yet' counts as no), or without daily return when a daily return is reasonable and no more than 60 non-return days are expected. Every other case, and every other country, keeps the normal tariff. ([Employees living abroad (Germany, Liechtenstein)](feature.md#SEC-07-living-abroad))
- **Liechtenstein: in principle no source tax liability** – An employee living in Liechtenstein who returns daily, or expects no more than 45 work-related non-return days, is in principle not liable to source tax, subject to the double taxation agreement. With more than 45 days the normal tariff code applies. ([Employees living abroad (Germany, Liechtenstein)](feature.md#SEC-07-living-abroad))
- **Source tax tariff letter** – Married/registered partners: C if the spouse works, else B. Single, divorced, separated or widowed: H with an H child, else A. If the cross-border tariff applies the letter becomes A→L, B→M, C→N, H→P. ([Source tax tariff basics and computed result](feature.md#SEC-05-tariff-basics))
- **Source tax result** – For every employee subject to source tax Lonio computes the Zurich tariff code = tariff letter + child digit + church-tax suffix (e.g. C1Y, L0Y), replacing HR's manual calculator step; the result is recorded for HR. Liechtenstein residents with daily return or at most 45 non-return days get 'in principle no source tax liability, subject to the double taxation agreement' instead, flagged for HR. Liechtenstein residents with more than 45 days get the normal code (the calculator adds 'subject to the double taxation agreement'). It is derived again from the latest answers whenever they change. ([Source tax tariff basics and computed result](feature.md#SEC-05-tariff-basics))
- **Permit reminder** – At the end of the questionnaire every non-Swiss employee and every employee living abroad (whatever their nationality, since it is not asked of them) is reminded to bring their permit on the first working day. HR words the reminder so that a Swiss citizen living abroad sees it does not apply to them. No data is collected. ([Completing and submitting the questionnaire](feature.md#SEC-08-completion-and-submission))
- **Discard answers that no longer apply** – If the employee changes an earlier answer so that later questions no longer apply, those answers are discarded on submission (data minimisation); until submission they may be kept so that going back and forth loses no input. After submission the questionnaire is read-only for the employee; later changes are made by HR. ([Completing and submitting the questionnaire](feature.md#SEC-08-completion-and-submission))
- **Content of the employee's copy** – If the employee asks for a copy, it is sent to the email address they typed in and shows the answers exactly as submitted, nothing more - no derived values such as the insurance start date or tariff code. ([Completing and submitting the questionnaire](feature.md#SEC-08-completion-and-submission))
