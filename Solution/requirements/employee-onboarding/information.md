every stakeholder. This view answers, for each piece: what, when we ask, when
it is required, what it depends on, and who needs it.

## Derived results (never asked)

- **Insurance start date** – The pension fund insurance starts on the first working day if that is the 1st of a month; otherwise on the 1st of the following month. Always derived from the latest first working day. ([First working day and insurance start date](feature.md#SEC-02-start-date-insurance))
- **Civil status for the pension fund** – The civil status is entered at the pension fund from its list. Provisionally, registered partnership and separated are entered as Married. ([Civil status and spouse for the pension fund](feature.md#SEC-03-civil-status-spouse))
- **Civil status for source tax** – For source tax, married and registered partnership count as 'married or registered partnership'; single, divorced, separated and widowed count as 'single'. ([Civil status and spouse for the pension fund](feature.md#SEC-03-civil-status-spouse))
- **Spouse exemption from source tax** – A married or registered-partner employee is exempt from source tax if the spouse/partner is Swiss or holds a C permit and lives in Switzerland. Separated employees count as single and get no exemption. ([Source tax liability check](feature.md#SEC-04-source-tax-liability))
- **Subject to source tax** – An employee living abroad is always subject to source tax, whatever the nationality. An employee living in Switzerland is subject only if not a Swiss citizen, holding no C permit and not exempted by a Swiss / C-permit spouse living in Switzerland. Only employees subject to source tax are asked the source tax questions. ([Source tax liability check](feature.md#SEC-04-source-tax-liability))
- **Source tax state of residence** – The calculator's residence answers are derived from the home address country: Switzerland, Germany, Liechtenstein, or another country. Switzerland and another country leave the base tariff unchanged. ([Employees living abroad (Germany, Liechtenstein)](feature.md#SEC-07-living-abroad))
- **Church-tax suffix** – Y for Roman Catholic, Christian Catholic or Evangelical Reformed; N for none or other. ([Source tax tariff basics and computed result](feature.md#SEC-05-tariff-basics))
- **Married tariff letter** – For married / registered-partner employees: C if the spouse is employed or receives replacement income, otherwise B. ([Source tax tariff basics and computed result](feature.md#SEC-05-tariff-basics))
- **Child result (per child)** – For each child the answers decide whether the child counts for the tariff, and for single (incl. divorced, separated, widowed) employees whether it counts with H. Rows marked inferred were not observed in the calculator and must be checked (OQ-calculator-coverage). Expression describes one child. ([Children for the source tax tariff](feature.md#SEC-06-children))
- **Child digit of the tariff code** – The digit equals the number of children that count (with or without H). No children or none counting gives 0. ([Children for the source tax tariff](feature.md#SEC-06-children))
- **Single-parent H tariff** – A single, divorced, separated or widowed employee with at least one child counting with H gets the H tariff. ([Children for the source tax tariff](feature.md#SEC-06-children))
- **Cross-border tariff applies** – For employees living in Germany the cross-border tariff applies with daily return and a certificate of residence ('not yet' counts as no), or without daily return when a return is reasonable and not more than 60 non-return days are expected. All other cases use the normal tariff. ([Employees living abroad (Germany, Liechtenstein)](feature.md#SEC-07-living-abroad))
- **Liechtenstein: in principle no source tax** – For employees living in Liechtenstein, daily return or not more than 45 non-return days gives 'in principle no source tax liability, subject to the double taxation agreement' instead of a tariff code, flagged for HR. More than 45 non-return days gives the normal tariff code subject to the double taxation agreement. ([Employees living abroad (Germany, Liechtenstein)](feature.md#SEC-07-living-abroad))
- **Tariff letter** – Married/registered partnership: C (spouse employed) or B; single etc.: H (single-parent) or A. With the cross-border tariff the letter becomes A→L, B→M, C→N, H→P. ([Source tax tariff basics and computed result](feature.md#SEC-05-tariff-basics))
- **Source tax result** – For every employee subject to source tax Lonio computes the tariff code = tariff letter + child digit + church-tax suffix (e.g. C1Y, L0Y) and records it for HR. Liechtenstein residents with daily return or at most 45 non-return days get 'in principle no source tax liability, subject to the double taxation agreement' instead, flagged for HR. Always recomputed from the latest answers. ([Source tax tariff basics and computed result](feature.md#SEC-05-tariff-basics))
- **Permit reminder** – At the end of the questionnaire, every non-Swiss employee and every employee living abroad is reminded to bring their permit on the first working day. HR words it so a Swiss citizen living abroad sees it does not apply to them. It collects no data. ([Completing and submitting the questionnaire](feature.md#SEC-08-completion-and-submission))
- **Discard answers that no longer apply** – If the employee changes an earlier answer so that later questions no longer apply, those answers are kept while the employee goes back and forth but discarded on submission. After submission the questionnaire is read-only for the employee; changes are made by HR and all derived results are derived again from the latest answers. ([Completing and submitting the questionnaire](feature.md#SEC-08-completion-and-submission))
